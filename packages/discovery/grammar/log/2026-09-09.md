**Fixed while building content**
- Register mismatch was assuming formal is wrong. It isn't — it depends on what was asked for, and
  the job wasn't carrying that. Worse, my first fix asked for *one* register across nine languages,
  which is the exact modelling error the brief warns about: the same warmth reads as respectful in
  Tamil and presumptuous in Bengali. Registers are now requested per language, and Bengali asks for
  formal. Two false flags disappeared with it.
- The gallery caught the content surface rendering **no errors at all** — with an unsupported
  language the whole board was blank, which is the worst possible answer to "why is nothing here".
- The bundle's `meaning_drift` fault marks every variant, which makes the headline detection
  experiment trivial: nine flags and she cannot miss them. Added `planted_drift_one`, which plants
  in exactly one language she cannot read. The original stays, since it's theirs.
- Four flags on one row squeezed the Tamil to one word per line. The flags column is capped now.

**Pattern changes**
- *Latency Waterfall* v1 → v1.1. v1 had a single sample. What broke: the pattern's own rule says
  distribution not one sample, and there was no event that could carry one. v1.1 adds
  `latency.distribution` with p50/p95 and a required `source: 'measured' | 'placeholder'`. Cost: one
  more event, and the UI now has to say out loud when its numbers are invented — which it should.
- *Back-translation Mirror* v1 → v1.1. v1 had `backtranslation` with sentence-level drift. What
  broke: a dropped regulated clause is not a rephrasing, and `removed` on a sentence diff buries the
  legal failure in a typographic one. v1.1 adds `clause.dropped` with a `regulated` flag, ranked
  above everything else. Cost: one more event and a second place to look for meaning loss.
- *Confidence Without Numbers* v1.1 → v1.2. The two-axis rule held, but nothing said what to do
  when the two axes disagree and the surface has to *rank* nine of them. v1.2 says the ranking is
  derived, never named — and the derivation is a named function in the reducer so it can be argued
  with rather than tuned in a component.
- *Commit Boundary* v1 → v1.1. v1's `checkpoint` had no state, so `CHECKPOINT_PARTIAL` from the
  brief was unrepresentable. v1.1 adds `checkpoint.state`. Cost: nothing yet.

**Fixed while building doc**
- The dim that makes one region readable was dimming that region too, which is the pattern
  inverted. The lit line is now exempt.
- The conflict field said "it has nothing else to offer" while showing two candidates, and the
  source pane said "no location for this value" when there were two locations in two other
  documents. Both were lies of omission in the honest-sounding direction.
- The accept gate hardcoded "22 values" while the document has 21. A wrong number in the sentence
  that describes a credit decision is the worst place in the surface to be sloppy.
- The fault toolbar offered coding's faults on the doc surface — a toolbar that does nothing when
  you click it, which is worse than no toolbar. It now takes the surface's own list.

**Open questions**

  someone who can't see the highlight? The surface's whole claim — verification is cheap — is
  conditional on sight, and nothing on screen admits that. Written up with three options.
- Three accessibility ones from coding, written up in `docs/accessibility-open-questions.md`: what a live region
  announces during a stream, what the tab order through a plan is, and what happens to focus when
  the agent blocks. Each has a recommendation and none is decided.
- Steering currently derives kept/discarded from checkpoint cleanliness. Predictable, and possibly
  too crude — if a person expects "keep the good work" to mean something semantic, this will feel
  arbitrary. Worth putting in front of the three developers.
- The `speculative` treatment (dashed spine, lower contrast) reads as provisional to me. It's also
  the exact treatment that could read as *disabled*. Needs a real test.
