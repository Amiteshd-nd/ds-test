# teaser

The Plinth product teaser, rendered entirely from code. A 1920×1080 HTML mockup of the
product (chat panel on the right, drafting canvas on the left) plays three chat sessions
back to back, each a "New chat" click away and each faster than the last: a 3BHK home
(prompt, thinking, the plan drawing itself, an edit that moves a wall), a master-bathroom
detail (tiles, drain, callout), and a 20-bed hospital ward that ends on "Looks good,
export". The camera pushes in on each prompt and each drawing and pulls wide between
sessions, then the screen fades to the wordmark and the waitlist line. Playwright captures
it frame by frame, ffmpeg stitches the frames with a synthesised soundtrack, and an
optional Gemini-generated hero still opens and closes the film. About 81 seconds.

Outputs: `out/teaser-1080p.mp4`, `out/teaser-4k.mp4`, and a `-silent` version of each.

```bash
corepack pnpm --filter teaser render          # the whole pipeline, about 6 minutes
corepack pnpm --filter teaser preview         # http://127.0.0.1:6300/?scrub=1 to scrub the timeline
```

## Layout

| Path | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `demo/script.json` | Every string and every timestamp. Edit this to change copy or pacing. |
| `demo/index.html` | The mockup. One paused GSAP timeline (`window.tl`), `window.seek(seconds)` renders any instant. |
| `demo/plans/*.svg` | One plan per scene, hand-laid (home at 1 ft = 20 px, bath at 50 px, ward at 9 px), one `<g>` per layer in draw order. Areas and the scale are in each header comment. |
| `demo/fonts.css` | Inter, Instrument Serif and UnifrakturCook embedded as base64 (generated by `render/fonts.ts`). |
| `demo/vendor/gsap.min.js` | GSAP, copied from `node_modules/gsap/dist` so the page loads with no network. |
| `render/capture.ts` | Playwright: seek, wait a frame, screenshot, for every frame. |
| `render/audio.ts` | Synthesises `out/audio.wav` from the timestamps in `script.json`. |
| `render/hero.ts` | Calls Gemini once for `assets/hero.jpg` when a key is available. Skips cleanly otherwise. |
| `render/assemble.sh` | ffmpeg: frames to H.264 at 1080p and 4K, hero zoom in/out with cross-dissolves, loudness-normalised AAC. |
| `render/check.ts` | Writes `out/verify-first.png`, `-middle`, `-last` from the finished 1080p file. |
| `render/pipeline.ts` | Runs the steps above in order. `pnpm render` calls this. |
| `render/serve.ts` | A tiny static server for `demo/` (Chromium will not let a `file://` page fetch its siblings). Ports 6300–6399. |

## Changing copy or timings

Everything lives in `demo/script.json`. The film is `scenes`, played in order; each scene
is one chat session with its own plan, and every time inside a scene counts from that
scene's start, so scenes can be reordered, dropped or added without retiming the others.
Per scene:

- `prompt`, `greeting`, `statusLines`, `reply`, `table`, `pills` are the conversation.
  `fileName` is what the toolbar shows. `plan` is the SVG.
- `t.*` is when each beat happens: cursor to the input, focus, typing start, send, thinking,
  draw, reply, table, pills, cursor to the pill, click, end. `layers.*` are the draw
  durations per plan layer (keyed by the `<g>` id; layers not listed take one second).
  The reply streams at `replyWordsPerSecond`; typing uses `typing.msPerChar` with
  `typing.jitterMs` of seeded jitter, so the rhythm is identical on every render and the
  typewriter strikes land on the same instants.
- `click` is optional: which pill the cursor clicks and what follows. `action: "edit"`
  tweens every element of the plan with a `data-after` attribute to those attributes and
  counts the listed `counters` (numbers, or `format: "ftin"` for feet and inches);
  `action: "export"` shows a toast on the canvas. Both stream `reply` at `replyAt`.
- `camera` is the list of camera moves for the scene (see below).

`transition` sets the New chat beat between scenes: the cursor travels to the button, clicks,
the session and plan clear, and the next scene starts after `settle`. `outro` sets the hold
before the fade to brand and the hold after it; the film's length follows from all of this
and is read from the page by the capture.
- `camera` (per scene) is the list of camera moves: each has `at`, `duration`, `scale` and
  a `focus` (`{el}` a CSS selector, looked up in the scene's session and plan first;
  `{svg: [x, y]}` a point in that scene's plan units; or `{x, y}` screen pixels; omit it to
  centre). The whole UI is scaled and panned so the focal point sits at the centre, clamped
  so the frame never shows past the UI's edge. Scale 1 is the full view. Because every
  frame is re-rendered from the DOM, a pushed-in shot is as sharp as a wide one. Delete the
  array for a static scene.
- `product.*` sets the wordmark, the domain on the end card and the brand colour it fades to.
- `video.*` sets fps, duration and size. `audio.*` sets gains and the loudness target.

Then `pnpm render`. To check a change without rendering, run `pnpm preview`, open the URL
with `?scrub=1`, and drag the bar (arrow keys step one frame, shift-arrow steps a second,
space plays).

## Changing the plan

Each plan in `demo/plans/` is authored by hand at the scale its header comment states.
Top-level `<g>` layers draw in document order; the layer with id `labels` fades its
`g.label` groups in one by one, every other layer draws its strokes at constant pen speed,
reveals white "gap" strokes and fills when their group starts, and fades text in. Dashed
strokes are revealed rather than drawn. If a room changes, update the header comment and
the matching numbers in that scene's `script.json` entry (`table`, `reply`, `click`).

## Determinism

Nothing on the page uses `setTimeout`, `requestAnimationFrame` loops or CSS transitions.
GSAP runs with `lazy: false`, so a `seek` writes every value synchronously; counters and
the pen crosshair are derived from the timeline's time inside `window.seek`. The capture
splits frames into contiguous slices across workers and each worker only seeks forward
from zero, so GSAP's start values are always recorded in order. Fonts are embedded, so
`document.fonts.ready` resolves without the network (the Google Fonts `<link>` is still
there for a browser preview; the embedded rules come last and win).

## Pipeline details

- **Capture** renders at `deviceScaleFactor: 2` (3840×2160) once; the 1080p file is a
  lanczos downscale of the same frames, which is both faster than a second pass and
  cleaner than native 1080p text. `--scale 1` captures a native 1080p pass instead.
  `--workers N` (default: half the cores, max 6), `--format jpeg` for quality-95 JPEGs if
  PNGs are too slow, `--from`/`--to` for a slice.
- **Audio** is pure synthesis in Node (no sox). Its cues are the timeline's own: the capture
  writes `out/events.json` (every keystroke, send, click and chime instant) from the page,
  so sound and picture cannot drift. Each keystroke is a mechanical typewriter strike (a
  hard transient, a metallic ring, a short thud), each send a carriage-return bell and
  slide, each pill or New chat click a soft pop, and a two-note chime marks a finished plan,
  a landed edit and an export. All of it sits well under the bed. Under it sits a
  synthesised bed in the register of product-launch films: four warm chords (Cmaj9, Am9,
  Fmaj7, G6) on detuned pads, a light plucked arpeggio and a soft sub, at 92 bpm, faded in
  and out. Gains are in `audio.*`. To use a real track instead, drop it at
  `assets/music.mp3` (or `.wav`, `.m4a`, `.aac`): assembly trims it to the film, fades it
  out, mixes it under the effects at `audio.musicGainDb` and skips the synthesised bed.
  Either way a two-pass `loudnorm` brings the mix to `audio.targetLufs` (−18) before AAC.
- **Hero** needs `GEMINI_API_KEY` in the environment, in `packages/teaser/.env.local`, or
  in `packages/landing/.env.local`. With a key, `assets/hero.jpg` is generated once at
  16:9 and kept; `--force` regenerates. With the hero present the film opens on a 3 s slow
  zoom-in and closes on a 3 s zoom-out, each cross-dissolved 0.5 s with the UI, and the
  audio is delayed to match. Without a key the film starts on the UI.
- **Encoding** is libx264, crf 18, yuv420p, 60 fps, `+faststart`. The frame sequence is read
  with `-reinit_filter 0` and pinned to rgb24: Chromium saves some frames with an alpha
  channel, and ffmpeg would otherwise rebuild the filter graph at the switch and reset the
  cross-fades (the symptom is a film that ends early). The film is built once
  at 4K in one filter graph and split into both encoders, so frames are decoded once.

## Timings on an M-series laptop

| Step | Time |
| --- | --- |
| Capture, 4836 frames at 4K, 6 workers | about 8 min |
| Assemble (one pass: 4K + 1080p encodes, audio) | about 3 min |
| Whole pipeline | about 12 min |

`pnpm render -- --hw` swaps libx264 for Apple's VideoToolbox encoder when the ffmpeg
build has it (bitrate-driven rather than crf, several times faster).

The frames (about 1.3 GB of PNGs) stay in `frames/` until the next render; `pnpm clean`
removes them with the outputs.

## Repo notes

This package animates with GSAP rather than `@cloud-march/motion`, on purpose: the teaser is
a render pipeline that needs a single scrubbable timeline, not a product surface, and the
brief specifies GSAP. Nothing here ships to a browser a visitor sees.
